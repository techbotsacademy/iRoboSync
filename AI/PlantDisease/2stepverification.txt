Google 2-Step Verification
Step-by-step guide for the ESP32 Plant Disease Detection project
Purpose
Enable Google 2-Step Verification on the Gmail account that will send email notifications from the ESP32 project. After verification is enabled, you can create an App Password if your Google Account supports this feature.
Part 1 — Turn on 2-Step Verification
Open Google Account Security: Visit https://myaccount.google.com/security and sign in to the Gmail account you intend to use.
Find the sign-in settings: Under “How you sign in to Google,” select “2-Step Verification.” The wording or page layout may vary.
Start setup: Select “Turn on 2-Step Verification” or “Get started,” if shown.
Verify your identity: Enter your Google Account password if requested, then follow the on-screen verification instructions.
Choose a verification method: Use an available method, such as a Google Prompt, authenticator app, passkey, or phone verification, as offered by your account.
Finish setup: Follow the remaining prompts and confirm that 2-Step Verification is turned on.
Part 2 — Create an App Password (if available)
An App Password is a separate 16-character code used by some apps or devices that cannot use Google’s usual sign-in flow. It is not your normal Google Account password.
Open the App Passwords page: Visit https://myaccount.google.com/apppasswords and sign in again if asked.
Create a password: If the option is available, enter a label such as “ESP32 Plant Disease Detector” and select Create/Generate.
Store it securely: Copy the generated code and keep it private. Use it in the ESP32 email configuration where the SMTP password is required.
Important security notes
Never share your App Password or include it in screenshots, public repositories, or messages.
If an App Password was exposed in previously shared code, revoke it and generate a new one.
Some account types or security configurations may not offer App Passwords. If the option is missing, review your account’s security settings or your organization’s administrator policy.
Successful SMTP submission does not guarantee that an email reached the recipient’s inbox. Verify the destination email address and check Spam/All Mail.
